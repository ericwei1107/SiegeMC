# Design: Infinite Team Potion Storages

Generated by office-hours on 2026-08-28  
Status: IMPLEMENTED IN WORKING TREE — pending live staging verification  
Mode: Builder

## Problem Statement

Siege players need a low-friction, public base supply of a particular potion for each side. A physical double chest should let a teammate take what they need, then be ready for the next teammate. The design must protect normal pre-existing potion chests, avoid concurrent access races, and be easy for operators to set up in-game.

## What Makes This Cool

The chest stays recognizably Minecraft: it is a visible base fixture, labelled with its potion, works through the normal inventory screen, and feels inexhaustible without a shop/menu detour. The one-at-a-time lock is quiet unless it matters, when the waiting player sees a private red message rather than confusing contents.

## Premises

1. Supply behavior must be opt-in and tied only to explicitly registered physical double chests; ordinary chests remain vanilla.
2. A sample potion inside the chest is the fastest reliable configuration interface, provided registration rejects mixed or non-potion contents.
3. A vanilla 54-slot inventory with a server-side holder lock is preferable to a custom GUI for the first public-base-supply release.
4. A native `TextDisplay` label is sufficient; no external hologram plugin is needed.
5. The registry should be configuration-backed now and map-aware so future map rotation can resolve supplies to the active copied map.

## Approaches Considered

### A. Config-backed physical double chest — selected

An explicit double chest stores one exact potion template and a team. On eligible open it fills all 54 slots; on close it refills and releases the lock. A native floating label names the team and potion.

- Effort: M
- Risk: Low
- Pros: Preserves vanilla chest feel; setup is sample plus one command; no extra dependency; narrow, reversible state.
- Cons: Must comprehensively block deposit/hopper loopholes; a chest has only 54 visible slots per visit; map identity needs a migration path.
- Reuses: Existing `/siege admin` command pattern, configuration handling, permissions/team state, and inventory event conventions.

### B. Virtual supply inventory behind a chest

Opening a registered chest presents a custom 54-slot supply inventory instead of the physical chest inventory.

- Effort: M
- Risk: Medium
- Pros: Stronger isolation from chest/hopper mechanics; simple fixed display.
- Cons: Loses the normal-chest feeling and produces more special-case UI behavior.
- Reuses: Existing custom inventory listener patterns.

### C. Per-map database supply stations

Store generalized stations, inventory policies, and item templates in the match/map database, then dynamically materialize them as maps rotate.

- Effort: L
- Risk: Medium
- Pros: The most extensible model for future map-specific supply rules.
- Cons: Overbuilds a single-potion public chest and introduces migration/lifecycle coupling before map rotation exists.
- Reuses: SQLite persistence patterns and the future map registry.

## Recommended Approach

Implement Approach A. It minimizes friction between fights, retains player expectations around real chests, and keeps the initial feature independent of the still-planned map-rotation system.

## Selected Behavior

- An admin targets a double chest whose non-empty slots all contain the same valid potion item and runs `register <red|blue>`.
- The exact item form and metadata are saved as the template. The storage fills all 54 slots with copies of it on eligible open and close.
- Only the owning team may withdraw during an active siege; a defined operator bypass is allowed. Opponents and spectators are denied.
- One player UUID holds a storage at a time. A second player receives: `This potion storage is currently in use.` in red chat.
- Withdrawals and shift-click out are allowed. Every insertion/swap/drag into the chest and all hopper transfers are blocked.
- The storage has a native `TextDisplay` label above its centre. It is recreated/restored from the registry and removed on unregister.
- Lock cleanup covers close, disconnect, death, inventory replacement, world unload, and plugin shutdown. Registered chest break is denied to ordinary players.
- `unregister` removes management and the label but leaves the chest's current contents as an ordinary chest.

## What I noticed

The priority was not unlimited items in the abstract; it was experienced players having “minimizing friction between fights.” You also caught the key safety concern early: preserving pre-existing storages. Choosing a labelled, public base chest makes the feature legible to teammates without turning it into another custom menu.
